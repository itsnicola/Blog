Is Istanbul expensive? Well in our experience, it depends. If you have done any research into travelling to Istanbul and Türkiye then you’ve probably seen many posts and content about the rising prices. I could yap for a long time  about the series of fiscal policy decisions that threw the Turkish economy into an ongoing crisis in 2018, but that isn’t really the point of this post. However, I think it’s important to acknowledge that since 2018, Türkiye has struggled with loss of value of the Turkish Lira, high inflation costs and so on, which now mean that locals now struggle to meet the basic costs of living, as prices continue to increase. 

With the rising costs, even with a stronger currency than the Lira, some things can be quite expensive, but we found that the prices were slightly less or comparable to New Zealand prices (not great for locals, but we are privileged to say that it was manageable for us). 

So, is it still possible to have a budget holiday in Türkiye? Yes! 
But don’t stay in Fatih – this neighbourhood is almost entirely tourists because that is where the majority of the tourist attractions are, and prices are reflective of this. I'll discuss later on [other options to stay](#where-to-stay).

The most important thing is to understand the currency and how that fits in your budget.

We had a food budget of about NZD$50 a day or roughly 25 Euros. That converts to 1200 Lira per day (at the time of writing - again, the Lira is a bit unstable). We found that once we had this budget in mind, it made Lira mean something to us so we could be selective about food options and choose things in our budget. And always check the menu prices first! We learned this the hard way, we paid 350 Lira each for Matcha, because it sounded cheap but it was actually double what we would have paid for at home - eeep! Once we understood the currency ie that 350 Lira = NZD$15 we did not make this mistake again.

We were able to stretch this budget quite far, occasionally a sweet treat or two would push it over but for the most part we ate pretty well, we had big turkish breakfasts, many kebabs (guilty as charged). I am still dreaming of the Kumpir.

### Where to Stay: 

The main touristy area is around Fatih, and the prices are very very high here, I would avoid staying or eating in this location. Public transport in Istanbul is cheap and easy and we used this almost exclusively.  

 We first stayed in a hostel near the Galata tower in the Beyoğlu district. I would highly recommend staying in this neighbourhood if you wish to be close to all the tourist attractions, without breaking the bank. The Fatih district is a quick walk or tram ride away. 

 I would avoid eating at any of the streets that directly access the Galata tower but if you go one or two streets back you will find more affordable options. 

We also had one night on the Asian side of the city, there were less tourists around and it felt ‘cool’ and edgy but not cool enough to be openly queer (still got looks for linking arms in the street though so don’t do that) in Kadikoy which I would also highly recommend, and if I return to Istanbul this is where I would stay. This neighbourhood was lively and there were less tourists around. Which in turn meant we found many affordable food options. 

In general public transport was easy and convenient, we had heard stories of taxi scammers so we were nervous to use them, but we had great success using the app similar to uber; bitaski. 

### The Attractions: 
Controversial (probably) the only tourist attraction we went to was the free blue mosque (and a non remarkable museum). The free blue mosque was beautiful, but we lasted about ten minutes in inside before getting bored which fully justified our decision to pay for any more attractions.

The prices of the main attractions in Istanbul are really expensive and while I don’t doubt their worth, the price is not something that we could justify. Besides, neither of us are huge museum and monument people, while we are interested in the history we find it gets a bit same same after a while. 

For example, entrance tickets in the Topkali Palace is 62 Euros at the time of writing, which is double that of others Palaces in europe such as Palacio Real in Madrid or the Palace of Versailles. 

We still had extremely packed and fun days even without doing the main tourist attractions, we certainly got our steps in!
